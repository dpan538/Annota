Predict–Reconcile Ink Compositor · 预测-校正墨迹合成器

Status: preview · version 1.2.1.2 · updated 2026-11-08 latencypredictionstylusconfidence

What it is. A self-written short-horizon predictor draws a confidence-weighted tail for the active stroke, then reconciles it when the real sample arrives; predictions never enter saved ink or PDF export.

Problem it solves

Reducing perceived pen-to-ink latency usually improves responsiveness but naive prediction overshoots on fast reversals and can corrupt the stroke.

How it works

A recency-weighted least-squares velocity and a quadratic curvature estimate extrapolate along an arc. Confidence combines a one-step backtest residual, turn quality, gap quality and look-ahead decay. Fast reversals fall back to one short low-confidence frame. When the real point arrives, the error is compared with a lead-scaled tolerance; large errors retract the prediction, otherwise the displayed point blends toward the truth. Only the active-stroke layer is drawn; Mark/Store/PDF are untouched.

Difference from existing approaches

The predictor is paired with an explicit reconciliation policy and confidence, rather than drawing predicted points as if they were real. This bounds overshoot and keeps the persisted stroke exact.

Limitations

  • Prediction is display-only and fades with confidence
  • A high-speed stop without a reversal sample can still show up to 64 px of one-frame tail
